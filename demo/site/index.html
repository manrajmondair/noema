A neural world model, with its limits visible

Two public benchmark results, a forecast of real human motor cortex you can check against the recording that followed, and three reviewed studies that failed their own prespecified criteria. Each is reported with the boundary of what it establishes.

What these do not establish

Both numbers say the model reconstructs and decodes well inside the distribution it was fitted on. Neither says it can forecast a region it has never seen. That is a separate claim, and §2 is where we tested it.

1 Forecast

Forecast

The model runs in the browser on thirteen recordings of one person's motor cortex, 176 channels, spanning thirty-nine days. Put a mark anywhere on a recording and it commits a forecast of the next 200 ms. The forecast is held alone, and then the recording arrives to check it. Nothing is selected for you and nothing is smoothed after the fact.

Correlation at 20 ms, per-channel mean removed 0.118 The headline. Roughly four times what repeating the last bin achieves.
Correlation at 200 ms, per-channel mean removed 0.051 Fifty-seven per cent of the skill is gone by the tenth step, and repeating the last bin has caught up.
Same forecast, mean left in 0.429 A forecast emitting nothing but each channel's long-run average scores 0.494 by this measure, beating the model on twelve of the thirteen recordings. That is why the centred number leads.

Open the forecast · 4.2 MB, self-contained, no network once loaded · runs on your machine, not ours

Two models ship, identical in every respect but the training objective: one predicts a single step ahead, the other is trained on its own ten-step rollouts. The difference between them is the only architectural claim on the page, and it appears entirely at long horizon — at one step they are indistinguishable, which is what should happen. The recordings shipped are session two of each day; the models read session one, and the two are checked for shared rows before either leaves Python.

2 Reviewed evidence

Reviewed evidence

Separate claims, separate tests. Benchmark scores establish reconstruction and behaviour decoding on public splits. They do not establish cross-region forecasting, so each study below keeps its own cohort, controls, uncertainty and decision, and none of them borrows support from the benchmarks above. None of the three supported the hypothesis it was designed to test. The intervals are shown at full width because they are the finding.
